# T-12 — Listener state, stated plainly

**Issue:** #12  **Commit:** 090e2e9  **Status:** shipped

## What shipped

The sixth window and beyond: role reads `Listening`, frequency is an em dash, status reads
`silent`, and the hint says *"The chord is full. This window listens, and will sing when another
one closes."* It renders all five sibling rules, counts itself in the voice count, and its
waveform sits at rest rather than the canvas being blank.

Closing a singer promotes it, with the ordinary 1.5 s attack.

## Decisions made

- **`say(line, visible)` gained a visibility argument, and the reason matters.** The hint retires
  permanently nine seconds after "Now move your windows." — but a window that has just fallen
  silent is owed an explanation regardless. A listener's line is always shown; the nudges obey
  `retired`. Without this a window that had been open a while would go silent with no
  explanation at all.
- **`retired` is separate from `lingering`.** `lingering` means the countdown has been scheduled;
  `retired` means it has fired. Collapsing them breaks the listener case.
- **The design's copy said "This tab listens".** Changed to "window", as everywhere else. The
  wording is otherwise the design's.
- **The listener's hint lags its role by one fade.** `say()` fades out before swapping, so any
  test asserting the listener copy has to poll rather than read once — that is the only thing
  that failed here, and the same trap will catch T-14.
- **`listening` is passed explicitly from `main.js`** rather than the overlay inferring it from
  `role === 'listener'`. The overlay does not import the roster vocabulary, and keeping it that
  way means it never needs to.

## Deliberately left

- **Reduced motion still animates the hint fade.** **T-14**.
- **The promotion path reuses the ordinary attack**, which the test pins by sampling the level
  before and after 1.4 s. No special-casing was needed — `update()` builds a `Voice` the same way
  whether it is the first one or a promotion.
